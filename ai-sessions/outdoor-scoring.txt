  Why this one: Duration matters more than amount: 2 mm spread over 6 hours
  ruins
  a day, while 2 mm in a 20-minute shower doesn't.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Signal: Wind
  Open-Meteo variable: wind_gusts_10m_max
  Why this one: Gusts are what you actually feel (umbrellas, cold, viewpoints).
  Mean wind speed understates it.
  ────────────────────────────────────────
  Signal: Sky
  Open-Meteo variable: sunshine_duration / daylight_duration
  Why this one: You already picked sunshine. Dividing by daylight gives a ratio,

  so a December day in Oslo isn't scored lower just because the day is short.

  Skip precipitation_probability_max. It has the same problem you found with
  weather_code: it's the highest hourly value of the day. One 70% hour at 3 am
  makes the whole day look like 70%. precipitation_hours is a better signal.

  Comfortable ranges (sub-score 0–1, linear between points)

  - Temperature (feels-like max): 1.0 from 18 to 26 °C, falling to 0 at 5 °C and
    at 35 °C.
  - Rain hours: 1.0 at 0 h, about 0.7 at 2 h, 0 at 8 h or more. Then multiply by
    an amount factor: no penalty under 1 mm, 0 at 15 mm or more.
  - Gusts: 1.0 below 30 km/h, 0 at 70 km/h.
  - Sunshine ratio: use it directly (0–1), maybe with a floor of 0.3 so
    overcast-but-dry isn't punished too hard. Overcast is still perfectly fine
    for walking around.

  Suggested weights: rain 40%, temperature 30%, wind 15%, sunshine 15%.

  Unfeasible days (hard gates → score 0)

  A day is unfeasible if any one of these is true:

  - Washout: precipitation_sum ≥ 20 mm or precipitation_hours ≥ 10.
  - Dangerous wind: wind_gusts_10m_max ≥ 75 km/h. That's roughly gale force,
    where things start falling and many countries issue wind warnings.
  - Heat stress: feels-like max ≥ 38 °C.
  - Severe cold: feels-like max ≤ −10 °C.
  - Thunderstorm: weather_code 95–99. This is the one place weather_code is
    useful. "Worst condition of the day" is bad for describing a typical day but
    is exactly right for a safety check. Caveat: a storm at 2 am also triggers
    it. You could make this a heavy penalty instead of a gate, or check hourly
    codes for daytime only.

  Gates matter because otherwise the factors compensate for each other: a
  perfect 22 °C day with 30 mm of rain would still score about 60%.

  One trade-off to decide

  Daily aggregates include the night, so night-time rain counts against the day.
  The precise fix is to fetch hourly data and aggregate only over roughly
  09:00–18:00 local time (Open-Meteo returns local time with timezone=auto). I'd
  stay on daily for now and list this as a known limitation, like the ski
  altitude one.

  Indoor sightseeing: this connects naturally to outdoor. Indoor is mostly "good
  regardless", with a bonus on days when the outdoor score is low or gated.
  That way it ranks highest exactly when outdoor fails.

  All the thresholds above are judgment calls, not standards. I can add them to
  the scoring decisions in DECISIONS.md as stated assumptions, or start

❯ Before that, about the temperature the sub-score hits 0 at 5 °C feels-like,
  and stays 0 all the way down to the -10 °C gate. A dry, sunny 5 °C day in

⏺ Yes, you're right. I'd lower the zero point all the way to the gate (−10 °C)
  rather than add a floor.

  Why the current curve is wrong. The problem isn't only that 5 °C scores too
  low. Everything between 5 °C and −10 °C scores the same 0, so a crisp 4 °C day
  and a brutal −9 °C day look identical. A sub-score should only reach 0 where
  the gate takes over. Otherwise there's a range where conditions keep getting
  worse and the score can't show it.

  Why not a floor. A floor removes the zero but keeps that flat range, just at a
  higher value. With a 0.3 floor, 4 °C and −9 °C would still tie. A floor works
  for sunshine because overcast skies have a bounded downside: a grey day is
  never worse than "grey". Cold keeps getting worse until it becomes dangerous,
  so the score should keep falling until the gate.

  What I'd pick:
  - Cold side: 1.0 at 18 °C, falling linearly to 0 at −10 °C. A dry, sunny 5 °C
    day in Paris then gets about 0.54 on temperature. With the 30% weight,
    that's a day score of roughly 86%. That's "good, bring a coat", which
    matches your intuition. At 0 °C it's about 0.36, and at −8 °C it's close to
    0, just before the gate.
  - Hot side: this has the same flaw. The sub-score hits 0 at 35 °C but the gate
    is at 38 °C, so I'd move the zero to 38 °C as well. That gives 1.0 at 26 °C
    down to 0 at 38 °C. The slope is steeper than the cold side (12 °C of range
    vs 28 °C), which fits: you can add layers against cold, but there's little
    you can do about heat.

  One side effect to know about: feels-like temperature already includes wind
  chill, so a cold, windy day is penalised twice, once in temperature and once
  in the wind sub-score. With 30% and 15% weights it's a mild effect, and I'd
  accept it rather than switch to raw air temperature. Raw temperature would
  lose the humidity effect on the hot side. I'll note it in DECISIONS.md
  alongside the wind thresholds when we write that up.

⏺ I'd multiply the whole day score by 0.75 when weather_code is 95–99.

  Why multiply instead of subtract. A storm costs you a part of the day, and how
  much that's worth depends on how good the day was. Multiplying keeps it
  proportional: a 90% day drops to about 68%, and a 40% day drops to 30%.
  Subtracting a fixed amount, say 20 points, would hit a mediocre day much
  harder (40% → 20%, half its value) and could go below zero.

  Why 0.75. The rain sub-score already counts the water. This penalty is for
  what a storm adds on top: you have to stop and get indoors because of
  lightning. NWS guidance is to go inside when you hear thunder and wait 30
  minutes after the last thunder before going back out. So a 30-minute Florida
  storm realistically costs about 1 to 2 hours of time outside. Against a
  sightseeing day of about 8 hours, that's roughly 15–25%. Daily data can't tell
  us when the storm happens, so I'd take the upper end of that range (25%) to
  account for the uncertainty.

  - Not 0.9: that's too small to change rankings. A storm day should clearly
    rank below an otherwise identical day without one.
  - Not 0.5: at that point it's nearly a gate again, which is what you wanted to
    avoid.

  A problem this brings up: the rain-amount rules. Florida storms are short and
  very heavy. 20 mm or more in 30 minutes is normal. So that day would still
  score 0 because of the washout gate (precipitation_sum ≥ 20 mm), even with the
  thunderstorm penalty instead of a gate. It's the same kind of mistake: one
  short event counted as if the whole day were wet.

  Even under 20 mm, the rain-amount factor (0 at 15 mm) drives the rain
  sub-score close to 0. Example: a day that's perfect except for a 1-hour storm
  with 10 mm of rain. Rain-hours factor ≈ 0.85, amount factor ≈ 0.36, so rain
  sub-score ≈ 0.3. Day score ≈ 72%, and ≈ 54% after the storm penalty. That's
  too harsh for an otherwise sunny day.

  My suggested fix:
  - Washout gate: require duration as well as amount, e.g. precipitation_sum ≥
    20 mm AND precipitation_hours ≥ 4. A long soaking day is still gated, and a
    short heavy storm isn't.
  - Amount factor: lower its influence, e.g. make it reach 0 at 30 mm instead of
    15 mm. The hours factor already handles "how much of the day is wet".

  With both changes, the Florida example lands around 70–75%: a good day that
  you plan around the afternoon storm. That matches how people actually sightsee
  there.

  from NWS, the temperature curve, the thunderstorm penalty and the washout
  change. Each one will include the reasoning and the 30/30 rule.

  Final formula

  Inputs (Open-Meteo daily values, km/h, timezone=auto):
  - T = apparent_temperature_max
  - P = precipitation_sum
  - H = precipitation_hours
  - W = wind_speed_10m_max
  - G = wind_gusts_10m_max
  - S = sunshine_duration / daylight_duration
  - C = weather_code

  1. Gates. If any of these is true, the score is 0 (unfeasible):
  - H ≥ 10, or (P ≥ 20 and H ≥ 4): washout
  - W ≥ 64 (NWS High wind threat) or G ≥ 93 (NWS Extreme)
  - T ≥ 45 or T ≤ −10

  2. Sub-scores (0–1). All are piecewise linear and clamped to 0–1.
  - Rain = hoursFactor × amountFactor
    - hoursFactor: 1.0 at 0 h → 0.7 at 2 h → 0 at 10 h
    - amountFactor: 1.0 at ≤ 1 mm → 0 at 30 mm
  - Temperature:
    - T < 18: (T + 10) / 28
    - 18 ≤ T ≤ 26: 1
    - T > 26: (45 − T) / 19
  - Wind (gusts): 1.0 at ≤ 40 → 0.5 at 56 → 0 at 93
  - Sunshine = max(0.3, S)

  3. Combine:
  score = 0.40·rain + 0.30·temp + 0.15·wind + 0.15·sun
  if C in 95..99: score ×= 0.75      // thunderstorm penalty
  result = round(score × 100, 1)

  Every sub-score now reaches 0 exactly where its gate takes over, so there are
  no flat stretches left.

       44 +| `P` | `precipitation_sum` (mm) |                            
       45 +| `H` | `precipitation_hours` |                               
       46 +| `W` | `wind_speed_10m_max` |                                
       47 +| `G` | `wind_gusts_10m_max` |

       49 +| `C` | `weather_code` |                                      
       50 +                                                              
       51 +**Formula**                                                   
       52 +                                                              
       53 +1. Gates. Any of these makes the day unfeasible, score 0:     
       54 +   - `H ≥ 10`, or `P ≥ 20` and `H ≥ 4` (washout)              
       55 +   - `W ≥ 64` or `G ≥ 93` (dangerous wind)                    
       56 +   - `T ≥ 45` or `T ≤ −10` (dangerous heat or cold)           
       57 +2. Sub-scores, 0 to 1, piecewise linear between the points, cl
          +amped:                                                        
       58 +   - Rain = hoursFactor × amountFactor                        
       59 +     - hoursFactor: 1.0 at 0 h, 0.7 at 2 h, 0 at 10 h         
       60 +     - amountFactor: 1.0 at ≤ 1 mm, 0 at 30 mm                
       61 +   - Temperature: 0 at −10 °C, 1.0 from 18 to 26 °C, 0.3 at 35
          + °C, 0 at 45 °C                                               
       62 +   - Wind (gusts): 1.0 at ≤ 40, 0.5 at 56, 0 at 93            
       63 +   - Sunshine: `max(0.3, S)`                                  
       64 +3. Combine:                                                   
       65 +   ```                                                        
       66 +   score = 0.40·rain + 0.30·temp + 0.15·wind + 0.15·sun       
       67 +   if C in 95..99: score ×= 0.75   // thunderstorm            
       68 +   result = round(score × 100, 1)                             
       69 +   ```                                                        
       70 +                                                              
       71 +Every sub-score reaches 0 exactly where its gate takes over. O
          +therwise                                                      
       72 +there'd be a stretch where conditions keep getting worse but t
          +he score                                                      
       73 +doesn't move (an earlier version had the temperature at 0 from
          + 5 °C all                                                     
       74 +the way down to −10 °C, so 4 °C and −9 °C tied).              
       75 +                                                              
       76 +Worked example, Lisbon 2026-10-15 (live forecast: T 29.6, P 0,
          + H 0,                                                         
       77 +G 24.8, S 39,261 / 40,376 s, C 1): rain 1.0, temp 0.72, wind 1
          +.0,                                                           
       78 +sun 0.972 → **91.2**.                                         
       79 +                                                              
       80 +**Why these choices**                                         
       81 +                                                              
       82 +- **Feels-like max, not air temperature or mean.** Apparent te
          +mperature                                                     
       83 +  includes humidity and wind chill, and the max is the daytime
          + value; the                                                   
       84 +  mean is dragged down by the night. Side effect: a cold windy
          + day is                                                       
       85 +  penalised twice (temp and wind). Mild at 30%/15% weights, so
          + I accepted it.                                               
       86 +                                                              
       87 +- **Temperature curve has a knee on the hot side.** Cold falls
          + slowly                                                       
       88 +  (18 → −10 °C, ~0.036 per °C): a dry sunny 5 °C day in Paris 
          +is fine                                                       
       89 +  with a coat and scores 0.54 on temperature (day ≈ 86). Heat 
          +falls about                                                   
       90 +  twice as fast up to 35 °C (~0.078 per °C), because you can a
          +dd layers                                                     
       91 +  against cold but not much against heat, and a full day walki
          +ng at 35 °C                                                   
       92 +  feels-like is already hard. 35 → 45 °C is just a tail to the
          + gate.                                                        
       93 +  With a single straight line to 45 °C, a typical Miami summer
          + day                                                          
       94 +  (38.6 °C, dry, sunny) scored 80, almost the same as Paris at
          + 5 °C; with                                                   
       95 +  the knee it scores 75.8.                                    
       96 +                                                              
       97 +- **Heat gate at 45 °C is calibrated on data, not a standard.*
          +*                                                             
       98 +  My first gate was 38 °C. Open-Meteo's archive for Jul–Aug 20
          +26 showed                                                     
       99 +  that would zero most of a Florida summer:

      138 +                                                              
      139 +- **Thunderstorm is a penalty (×0.75), not a gate.** With dail
