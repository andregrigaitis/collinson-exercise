  1. Variables

  All names checked against the Open-Meteo Forecast API docs.

  ┌──────────────┬────────────────────────────┬────────────┐
  │     Use      │          Variable          │    Unit    │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Fresh snow   │ snowfall_sum (daily)       │ cm         │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Snow quality │ temperature_2m_max (daily) │ °C         │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Rain on snow │ rain_sum (daily)           │ mm         │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Lifts        │ wind_gusts_10m_max (daily) │ km/h       │
  ├──────────────┼────────────────────────────┼────────────┤
  │ Base depth   │ snow_depth (hourly only)   │ m (not cm) │
  └──────────────┴────────────────────────────┴────────────┘

  - Air temperature, not feels-like. What matters here is whether the snow is
    dry or slushy, and that depends on air temperature.
  - Rain is its own factor. Chamonix on 8 Jan had 17.7 mm of rain at a 3.5 °C
    max. Temperature alone wouldn't mark that as a bad day.
  - For snow_depth, use the daily minimum.
    - The hourly series is noisy. Zermatt on 6 Jan dropped from 42 to 28 cm with
      a −5.8 °C max and no rain, and Chamonix on 11 Jan swung from 29 to 44 cm.
      The daily max picks up those spikes.
    - The minimum means "covered all day". A snowy day's own snowfall is already
      scored by snowfall_sum, so using the minimum avoids counting it twice.
      The next day's minimum includes the new snow, so the day after a storm
      gets the credit.
    - I'd avoid "value at 09:00": the historical response reports
      utc_offset_seconds: 7200 for Europe/Paris in January, which looks wrong
      (it should be UTC+1), so a fixed hour could be off.
    - One edge case: the season's first snowfall onto bare ground is still
      blocked by the thin-base gate below on that day.

  3. Applicability

  Not applicable when every one of the 7 days fails the base gate (min depth <
  10 cm). Reason: "No snow on the ground this week".

  This is your idea with the gate's threshold instead of "> 0", and it's the
  better version. A week where every day is gated would be all zeros anyway, so
  this rule replaces exactly that case. With "> 0", a trace reading or a 2 cm
  dusting in Madrid would make skiing "applicable" and then score seven zeros.
  Fresh snowfall doesn't need its own check, because snow falling on bare ground
  is gated anyway.

  4. Weights

  Fresh snow 30%, temperature 25%, base 15%, wind 15%, rain 15%.

  - Fresh snow and temperature together are 55%, which keeps them as the main
    signal as already decided.
  - Base is 15% because depth is noisy and its real job is the gate.
  - Wind is 15% because closures are handled by the gate. Also, valley gusts
    understate the ridge: the highest gust in Chamonix all month was 51.5 km/h,
    so the gate never fired.
  - Rain is 15%, enough to sink a rainy day without zeroing it.

  The ranking makes sense. Things to know:

  - Chamonix early January is gated even though the slopes were open. That's the
    q3 limitation showing up as expected, and worth a line in DECISIONS.md.
  - A day with no new snow can't score above about 79, because 0 cm fresh still
    costs 21 points. Zermatt's whole month sits at 55–77, so the spread mostly
    comes from temperature. That's acceptable to me; lowering the fresh-snow
    weight would widen the spread.
  - Snowing days aren't penalised for poor visibility. Chamonix 10 Jan had 0%
    sunshine and still scored highest. I left sunshine out on purpose, because
    it works against the fresh-snow factor; that could go under "Cut".
